Agreement redlines: who can do what

Three things: what "D+" (the first step) means, how the signatory can bring in their own reviewers, and how Teton's side fits the permission matrix.

1 · What "D+" means

The redlining mock suggested two steps. The difference is where the typing happens. In step 1 (D+), everyone still edits in Word and the deal room handles the rest. In step 2 (A), people can also type their edits straight into the deal room.

1

Step 1 · "D+" = file rounds, with the changes read in

D = upload/download. The + = we read the tracked changes inside the file.
  1. 📄
    RevOps uploads the draftThe Word file goes to the Agreement tab. Word
  2. ✎
    Prospect's counsel downloads it and redlines in WordTrack Changes, comments: what lawyers already do. Word
  3. ⤒
    They upload the redlined fileThe deal room reads every tracked change and comment out of the file.
  4. ✓
    Teton reviews each change in the deal roomAccept, reject, Teton-only notes, approval flags: the review panel from the last mock. In the app
  5. ⤓
    Download Teton's responseA Word file with your decisions applied and counters as tracked changes, uploaded as round 3. Word
You getOne place for every round, a reviewable list of changes, internal notes, approvals, no lost email attachments.
You don't get yetTyping edits in the browser. Counter-wording is written in Word.
2

Step 2 · "A" = redline in the browser too

Everything in step 1, plus a Suggesting mode on the document.
  1. 📄
    RevOps uploads the draftSame as step 1. Word
  2. ✎
    Prospect types edits right in the Agreement tabLike Google Docs' Suggesting mode. Counsel can still use Word and upload instead. In the app
  3. →
    "Send redlines to Teton"No file needed. In the app
  4. ✓
    Teton accepts, rejects or types a counter in placeNo round trip to Word for counters. In the app
  5. ⤓
    Word file on demandWith tracked changes for counsel, or clean for DocuSign. Word
AddsFaster rounds for people who don't want to touch Word; counters without leaving the app.
CostsThe biggest build: our own editor, and the formatting limits mentioned before.
Analogy: step 1 is a shared mailbox with a smart sorter. Letters (Word files) still get written elsewhere, but every one lands in one place, already sorted into "changes to decide on". Step 2 lets people write the letter at the mailbox. Most of the value (one place, the review list, approvals) comes in step 1.

2 · Who on the prospect's side can see and redline

The signatory always has access. The rep can give access to others, and (if the rep allows it) the signatory can invite their own people: counsel, a CFO, an operations lead. Each person gets one of four levels.

4 · Teton's side, in the permission matrix

You said deal owner and admins by default, editable in the permission matrix. One wrinkle: "deal owner" is a relationship, not a role. A rep owns some deals and not others. So each cell in the matrix would say how far the permission reaches: All deals, Own deals (deals they own or collaborate on, your call), or none. Admin is all on and locked, as planned.

5 · Decisions

  1. Can the signatory invite reviewers without the rep approving each one?Recommended: yes, with the rep notified and able to remove them (a switch per deal to require approval).
  2. Only Lead reviewers send rounds to Teton?Recommended: yes, so Teton gets one consolidated set of redlines per round.
  3. What invited reviewers can seeRecommended: the Agreement tab only, unless the rep opens more.
  4. "Own deals": owner only, or owner and collaborators?Collaborators already exist on deals. Should a collaborating rep be able to accept changes?
  5. Is RevOps the same as Deal Desk?There's no RevOps role today. The matrix could rename Deal Desk to RevOps, or add RevOps as its own role.
